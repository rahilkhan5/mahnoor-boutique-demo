Scope: the whole boutique store demo: home with the collection (`src/pages/index.astro`), 16 product pages (`src/pages/p/[slug].astro`), the parcel page with cart and checkout (`src/pages/parcel.astro`) and order tracking (`src/pages/track.astro`). Mode: Persuade on the home page, Operate on the parcel and checkout.

Audience: boutique owners and home designers in Pakistan judging a demo on a phone; story visitors are women shopping for lawn, ready to wear and formals on a phone, often late at night, from any city.
Job: make a customer want a piece and then order it without a single DM. Action: browse, open a piece, pick size or stitching, add to parcel, check out with cash on delivery, JazzCash, Easypaisa or card, then track the order.
Proof: 16 pieces with honest photos, real PKR prices, stock, sizes, what is in the parcel, delivery days by city, a working cart and a checkout that clearly charges nothing.
Constraints: everything labelled demo, photos credited, no real numbers (03XX XXXXXXX), no card or wallet fields at all (payments are simulated with a plain demo step), orders and tracking are fake and stay on the device.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The store as the parcel on its way to you. Every piece wears a consignment label: its article code, its stitching spelled out, its price in the COD amount box and its delivery days by city, so ordering feels as certain as signing for the parcel at the door. It refuses the white fashion grid with a campaign banner, and the Instagram "DM for price" habit.

OWN-WORLD: Slip paper (#fbfaf5) printed in form blue (#1f3fbf) for labels, boxes and rules; ink (#141414) for what is filled in; courier orange (#c9380d) for one job only, the thing you are about to pay for (price boxes, Add to parcel, Place order); kraft parcel tape (#c9a77c) as section bands; the pink and yellow carbon copies as the only other panel colours. Sofia Sans Extra Condensed for printed form headings and big piece names, IBM Plex Sans for reading, IBM Plex Mono for filled-in data (codes, prices, quantities, tracking lines). The boutique's name stamped in Nastaliq like a rubber stamp. Barcodes drawn from each article code. Photos stay big and true in colour.

STORY: A customer lands on this week's piece, one piece per view, big, with its label stuck on the photo's corner. She sees the price and that it reaches her city in 2 to 3 days, opens it, picks M or adds stitching, presses Add to parcel (it stamps), checks out with cash on delivery, and gets a printed consignment note with a tracking line that prints each state. A boutique owner sees her collection selling itself.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Home: a slip-style header (Nastaliq stamp, Mahnoor Boutique, Lawn, Ready to wear, Formals, Track, Parcel with count). This week's piece as one big photo with the consignment label stuck across its lower corner: article code, three-piece unstitched, COD amount Rs 6,490, Lahore 1 to 2 days, Karachi 3 to 4 days, a barcode. Beside it, the drop ranked by weight: this week's piece name biggest, an Add to parcel button and a See the piece link. On phones: name, photo with label, button, in the first screen.

FORM: Cash on Delivery Slip, the assigned direction the user approved on my recommendation, seed key 4b042395. Raises carried: one piece per view with real empty space (ikebana); courier orange for the pay job only (film); order states print themselves as lines (terminal); prices in tabular figures in ruled boxes (exposure record); buttons press like a rubber stamp and leave a mark (cel); drops ranked by weight (poster). Signature interaction: Add to parcel presses like a stamp, leaves an "ADDED" stamp on the label, and the parcel count in the header ticks up.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
